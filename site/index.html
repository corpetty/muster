A local-first client on the Logos stack

Do things together, privately.

Muster (v.) — to assemble, as people gathering for a common purpose. Get people into a room, agree on what to do, and do it, without anyone outside the room watching.

Muster coordinates multi-party transactions inside end-to-end encrypted conversations. Pay from a shared account, split a bill, or request XMR. The conversation is the security boundary: who is in the room decides who can read what is being done.

The shape of the product

Start with what you want to do, not with a chat.

A chat app asks who? first. Muster asks what you are doing, and derives the rest: the room, the account, and what each person must bring.

01 · THE SHELL

Who you are, and what you reach

Your keys, your wallets, the plugins you trust, and the infrastructure you point at. RPC endpoints and store nodes are settings — untrusted and chosen by you. No server, no telemetry.

02 · THE INTENTION

Start something

Name what you want to do with a group — pay from a shared account, split a bill, request a payment in XMR. The intention opens the room and asks each person for exactly what it needs.

03 · THE ROOM

Proposals that carry their own proof

Every proposal is a card with its live state. Open it to see the exact bytes you would sign, the domain they bind to, and where each input came from.

The lifecycle is the curriculum

Every step of a transaction, made visible.

Muster's first job is education. It walks people through the whole lifecycle of a multi-party transaction, and at each step answers four questions on screen. The teaching client and the real client are the same client: there is no demo mode.

  1. draftSomeone writes what the group should do.
  2. proposedIt enters the encrypted room as a card.
  3. collectingMembers review the effect and contribute.
  4. executableThe driver's threshold is met.
  5. submittedIt leaves the room, onto a chain.
  6. settlingThe driver says what finality means.
  7. finalSettled, and rebuilt from the log alone.

Same step, different stack

Where the information goes.

Coordinating a shared transaction usually means a service in the middle: it holds the proposals, pools the signatures, and sees everything. Muster keeps that work inside the room.

Ten invariants

Breaking one is a bug, not a trade-off.

Ten rules the code holds, whatever the tests say. Each has a typed spec with acceptance oracles, and probes that run against the module core.

  1. Review the effect; sign the materialization.Your client re-derives the bytes from what you reviewed and refuses on mismatch. This check cannot be turned off.
  2. A signature is worthless anywhere else.Every signing payload commits to environment, account, slot and expiry.
  3. Plugins never sign.They hold no keys and touch no network. They emit typed blocks only.
  4. State = reduce(log).Nothing exists that cannot be rebuilt from the log and your keys. Reorder or duplicate events: the state converges.
  5. Deterministic bytes.The same value always encodes to the same bytes. Every hash is a domain-separated record.
  6. The core never interprets contributions.Drivers do. Rounds, threshold and finality are described by the driver, never hardcoded.
  7. Membership epochs are real.Adding a member re-keys forward. They cannot read earlier epochs.
  8. No server, no telemetry.Store nodes and RPC endpoints are untrusted, configurable infrastructure.
  9. You speak only for yourself.The client tells a member only what another member disclosed. Inside the room, every approval and decline is named.
  10. Nothing is signed whose inputs are unaccounted for.Each input to a signature has a recorded origin and log position. If any origin is unknown, signing is refused.

New · XMR in a room

Request XMR. The payee's own wallet confirms it.

Monero is fungible cash, and settlement stays on Monero. Muster covers the rest of the deal: agreeing in a private room, asking for the payment, and confirming it — with no new wallet app, and no custody.

  1. Agree in the roomThe terms and the request live in the encrypted room. The payee agrees to the request too: their word that the address in it is theirs.
  2. A fresh subaddressThe payee's own Monero wallet in Basecamp mints a subaddress for this request alone.
  3. Pay from any walletEach payer pays through a monero: link or QR code carrying the address and the exact amount — from Cake, Feather, or Basecamp's own wallet.
  4. Their own wallet confirmsThe payment counts when the payee's wallet sees exactly that amount arrive at that subaddress, at 10 confirmations. A payer's "I paid" never decides.
  • On chain · Monero's guaranteeNo sender, receiver or amountThe Monero chain shows none of them, and a subaddress cannot be linked to the payee's other addresses. That privacy is Monero's, not Muster's, and the card says so.
  • In the room · Muster'sThe deal stays insideThe terms, the memo and the transaction id stay in the encrypted room. The monero: link carries only the address and the amount.
  • Keys · none in MusterMuster holds no Monero key and never spendsIt asks the payee's wallet, through the platform's Monero modules, for a subaddress and for its history. Opening the wallet is the wallet app's job, and every payment is sent by the payer's own wallet.

Built, on stagenet

  • The request, a bill split in XMR, the link and QR code, "I paid", and the rule by which the payee's wallet confirms. Family monero.split; its seven guarantees are a typed spec, graded 7/7.
  • Seen on screen in Basecamp 0.3.2: a real stagenet wallet minting the request's subaddress, and the QR code reading back as the link, byte for byte.
  • Until a payment is confirmed automatically, the payee can mark it received by hand.

Still ahead

  • A live stagenet payment, confirmed by the payee's own wallet at 10 confirmations, end to end. So far that rule is held by tests over a stand-in wallet.
  • Paying from Basecamp's own wallet, once the wallet app can review a send another module prepared (an upstream ask).
  • Mainnet, behind a gate. A chain-side receipt needs Monero tx proofs upstream.

Monero multisig stays out: upstream calls it experimental, and the platform's wallet does not expose it. Every XMR action is each person paying their own part. The plan: Monero in rooms · the build: the labbook · the spec: derived-exo-dcc.5.

The whole transaction, link by link

All seven links are Muster's to cover.

A transaction between people leaks at seven links: discovery, diligence, negotiation, contracting, ordering, settlement, enforcement. Every one is in Muster's scope, at different stages. Each row says what the code does today; what is planned is named as planned.

Link
Muster today
Ahead
Discovery
A room starts from an invite, not a directory or a lookup. Nothing on a room's topic names its members. The store node still sees that someone asked to join.
Finding counterparties you don't already know, with no platform that learns who looked.
Diligence
Each member's two identities are bound by a signed statement. Members disclose their own accounts, and the client checks them against the chain. It says about a member only what that member disclosed.
Checks on a counterparty beyond what they disclose.
Negotiation
An end-to-end encrypted room with no coordination server. Proposals, declines and chat stay inside it. Adding a member re-keys forward.
Choice blocks, and offer and counter-offer.
Contracting
The client re-derives what it signs and refuses on a mismatch. Every signing payload commits to environment, account, slot and expiry. Signing is refused when any input's origin is unaccountable.
Showing what breaking a commitment would cost the party who could break it (proposed).
Ordering
Submission goes through infrastructure you choose, and the flow view names the RPC provider when a driver uses one. An XMR payer broadcasts through their own wallet and node. Muster can send a room's messages through the Logos mixnet. It is an opt-in setting, built on Logos Delivery 0.3's sender anonymity. It hides which participant sent a message, not who reads a room: reading still asks a store node for the room directly.
Hiding who reads a room. Submission through your own node or a proxy, rail by rail.
Settlement
The intent settles on the chain its driver names, and the card says what that chain reveals. A FROST spend shows one signature; a LEZ private split shows only that private transfers happened; a Safe or Bitcoin multisig is public; on Monero the chain shows no sender, receiver or amount — Monero's guarantee.
More rails.
Enforcement
The card names the rail, and so whose producers settle it. The chain enforces the multisig thresholds. Every room's record exports as a self-verifying proof. A split share — XMR included — counts as paid only when the person owed confirms it, and nothing yet makes anyone pay.
Escrow; swaps (RFP-003); a chain-side XMR receipt, once Monero tx proofs exist upstream.

Muster classifies; it never scores. A link is described by what holds it and whose conduct it still rests on, never by a percentage. The full table, with what each link leaks: explaining Muster, §4c.

The fourth question

What Muster does not hide — yet.

A client that ships a real improvement and names its remaining leak teaches more than one that claims the perfect stack already exists. Each gap names the fix and where that fix actually stands.

Partial

The store node sees the conversation graph

Content is encrypted. But a store node still sees a room's content topic, and the timing and size of what is published and fetched. It never sees who is in the room.

Partly addressed: Muster can send a room's messages through the Logos mixnet. It is an opt-in setting, built on Logos Delivery 0.3's sender anonymity. It hides which participant sent a message, not who reads a room: reading still asks a store node for the room directly.

Partial

Verified reads trust a state root

A verified balance checks a Merkle proof against a state root — which the client fetches from the RPC you configured, and trusts.

The fix: a beacon light-client sidecar that verifies the root itself. Deferred on infrastructure.

Not built

Nothing makes anyone pay their share

A split records who agreed and who paid, truthfully. But no contract holds the money: it is a promise the group can see, not one the chain enforces. An XMR request is the same.

The fix: a split with deposits, each share locked in escrow at agreement.

Install

Add Muster's catalogue to Basecamp.

Muster ships its own catalogue for Logos Basecamp. Add it once, then install Muster like any other app; Basecamp resolves every dependency from the official Logos catalogue. The first release is muster-v0.2.0.

Linux x86_64 onlyLogos Basecamp 0.3.2about 650 MB of downloadsunsigned
  1. Add the catalogue

    In Basecamp, open Settings → Package Repositories. Under Add a repository, paste this URL and press Add:

    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/corpetty/muster/main/catalog/logos-repo.json

    A Muster entry appears under Logos Official, enabled.

  2. Install Muster

    Open Applications, search muster, and click Muster. In the install dialog, untick Monero Node (local) unless you mean to run your own Monero node — the wallet can use a remote one. Keep the rest, then press Install.

  3. Open it

    Press Launch. Muster opens on Home, where you set up your accounts and chains (first-run runbook). Members of a room should run the same version.

What adding it trusts

Read this before you add the URL.

  • It publishes two packages, muster_module and muster_ui, and nothing else. Basecamp takes the newest version of a package across every catalogue you enable, so a catalogue that listed an official package's name could replace it. Muster's never does; its release script refuses to.
  • Every dependency comes from the official Logos catalogue, which Basecamp enables by default: Delivery, the LEZ core, the EVM and RLN modules, and the optional Monero wallet modules.
  • Nothing is signed. That is true of the official catalogue too. Basecamp checks each download against the hash the catalogue lists, so you get the file it names. Nothing proves who built it.
  • Muster runs inside your Basecamp session with no sandbox, like every Basecamp module. Adding a catalogue means trusting its publisher with that.
  • Linux x86_64 only. On other platforms Basecamp shows Muster as "Not Available".

Where it stands

Real, running, and honest about the rest.

Everything shown is enforced by running code. A Nim core behind a language-neutral contract, and a QML interface, built as Logos modules.

Runs today

  • The whole lifecycle in the UI — propose, approve, submit — against a real 2-of-3 Safe.
  • Two instances converge over the live Logos network: join, ask, admit, re-key.
  • A relaunched member re-enters its rooms and reads them again, its founding epoch included, on Logos Delivery 0.3.2.
  • Safe, Bitcoin, FROST and the LEZ multisig program, coordinated from the room.
  • Split the bill in ETH, tokens, Bitcoin, privately on the LEZ, and in XMR, where a one-payer split is a payment request (stagenet).
  • Muster can send a room's messages through the Logos mixnet. It is an opt-in setting, built on Logos Delivery 0.3's sender anonymity. It hides which participant sent a message, not who reads a room: reading still asks a store node for the room directly.
  • An install from Muster's own Basecamp catalogue.
  • All ten invariants, each held by probes in the test suite.

Still ahead

  • The multi-party runs across two machines: a Safe transaction settled over the live network, and resilience when a member drops mid-collection.
  • An XMR payment on stagenet confirmed by the payee's own wallet end to end; paying from Basecamp's own wallet; mainnet.
  • The LEZ multisig and a FROST ceremony, run across two instances.
  • Every LEZ path live on the v0.3 testnet.
  • Hiding who reads a room, and a plugin runtime beyond the sandbox.
